Editorial Method in the 1896 Apollonius¶

Whereas Euclid’s Elements required word-for-word translation to preserve axiomatic phrasing, Heath recognized in 1896 (Preface, pp. vii–xii) that Apollonius’s Conics—spanning 387 dense propositions of four-letter ratio manipulations without algebraic symbols—demanded a faithful edition in modern geometric-algebraic notation that:

  1. Retains 100% of Apollonius’s synthetic Euclidean proof steps without injecting anachronistic Cartesian coordinates or calculus;

  2. Consolidates parallel single-branch, opposite-branch, ellipse, and hyperbola repetitions into 147 numbered Propositions while indexing every classical Greek/Halley number in square brackets (e.g., Proposition 129 [VII. 12, 13, 29, 30]);

  3. Prefaces the treatise with a 170-page historical and analytical monograph (pp. xvii–clxx) reconstructing Menaechmus, Aristaeus, Euclid, Archimedes, Pappus, and Zeuthen's coordinate-transformation insights.
