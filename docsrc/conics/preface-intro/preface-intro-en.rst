Preface, Introduction & Prefatory Letters
=========================================

.. folio:: Heath 1896, pp. vii–xii, xvii–lxxxvi (PDF 15–20, 25–94)

1. From Three Right Cones to One Oblique Scalene Cone
-----------------------------------------------------

Prior to :person:`Apollonius of Perga <apollonius>`, Greek geometers generated the three conic sections by cutting three *distinct species of right circular cones* with a plane strictly *perpendicular to one generator* of the cone (:cite:`Heath 1896, pp. xvii–xxx <heath:1896>`):

* :person:`Menaechmus <menaechmus>` (c. 350 BCE), seeking two mean proportionals :eq:`a : x = x : y = y : b` to solve the Delian cube-duplication problem (:eq:`x^2 = ay`, :eq:`y^2 = bx`, :eq:`xy = ab`), cut a **right-angled**, **acute-angled**, and **obtuse-angled** right cone perpendicular to a slant side, calling the curves simply the *section of a right-angled cone* (*orthotome*), *section of an acute-angled cone* (*oxytome*), and *section of an obtuse-angled cone* (*amblytome*).
* :person:`Aristaeus the Elder <aristaeus>` (in his *Five Books on Solid Loci*) and :person:`Euclid <euclid>` (in his lost four-book *Conics*) preserved this three-cone triad, while :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` (*Quadrature of the Parabola*, *On Conoids and Spheroids*) already recognized that elliptical sections arise when an arbitrary plane cuts all generators of a cone or cylinder (:cite:`Archimedes, ed. Heath 1897 <archimedes:heath1897>`).

Apollonius revolutionized the science by proving in **Book I** that *every circular cone whatsoever—whether right or oblique (scalene)—contains all three species of conic sections together with the two-branched opposite sections*, solely by varying the inclination of the cutting plane relative to the axial triangle:

.. picture:: /_static/figures/fig_oblique_cone_sections.svg
   :alt: Oblique scalene double cone showing parabola, ellipse, and two-branch hyperbola sections

**Plate 1 — Apollonius's Oblique Double Cone (Book I.1–14)**. From apex :g:`A` over circular base :g:`BC`, a plane parallel to generator :g:`AC` cuts the :term:`parabola <parabole>`; a plane meeting all generators of one nape cuts the :term:`ellipse <elleipsis>`; and a plane meeting both napes cuts the :term:`hyperbola <hyperbole>` and its :term:`opposite branch <antikeimenai>`.

2. Apollonius's Own Prefatory Letters (Books I–VII)
---------------------------------------------------

.. folio:: Heath 1896, pp. lxix–lxxv (PDF 77–83)

Unlike Euclid's impersonal axiomatic treatise, Apollonius prefaced each Book of the *Conics* with an epistolary cover letter explaining its genesis, scope, and mathematical novelty (:cite:`Heiberg 1891 <heiberg:1891>`, :cite:`Halley 1710 <halley:1710>`, :cite:`Heath 1896, pp. lxix–lxxv <heath:1896>`).

General Preface (Book I — To Eudemus of Pergamum)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. epigraph::

   "Apollonius to :person:`Eudemus <eudemus-pergamum>`, greeting. If you are in good health and spirits, it is well; I too am moderately well. During the time I spent with you at Pergamum I observed your eagerness to become acquainted with my work in conics; I am therefore sending you the first book, which I have corrected, and I will forward the remaining books when I have finished them to my satisfaction. For I daresay you have not forgotten my telling you that I undertook the investigation of this subject at the request of :person:`Naucrates <naucrates>` the geometer, at the time when he came to Alexandria and stayed with me, and that, when I had worked it out in eight books, I gave them to him at once, too hurriedly, because he was on the point of sailing, and therefore they were not thoroughly revised—indeed I had put down everything just as it occurred to me, postponing revision till the end."

Apollonius then divides the entire **eight-book architecture** into two grand divisions:

1. **Books I–IV — Elementary Introduction** (*πέπτωκεν εἰς ἀγωγὴν στοιχειώδη*):
   - **Book I**: Modes of producing the three sections and the :term:`opposite branches <antikeimenai>`, and their fundamental properties (:term:`symptōmata <symptoma>`) worked out more fully and generally than in earlier writers.
   - **Book II**: Properties of diameters, axes, and :term:`asymptotes <asymptotoi>`, together with constructions of tangents.
   - **Book III**: Many remarkable theorems useful for the synthesis of solid loci and :term:`diorismoi <diorismos>`, the majority and prettiest being new—providing at last the complete synthesis of the :term:`Locus with respect to Three and Four Lines <topos-pros-treis-kai-tessaras-grammas>`, of which :person:`Euclid <euclid>` had worked out only a fortuitous fragment.
   - **Book IV**: In how many ways conic sections meet one another and the circumference of a circle, besides the behavior of double-branch hyperbolas left unproven by :person:`Conon of Samos <conon>` and :person:`Nicoteles of Cyrene <nicoteles>`.
2. **Books V–VIII — Advanced Special Investigations** (*περιουσιαστικώτερα*):
   - **Book V** (to :person:`Attalus <attalus>`, preserved via :person:`Thābit ibn Qurra's <thabit-ibn-qurra>` Arabic): Minimum and maximum straight lines (normals), treated for their own intrinsic beauty rather than mere tangent corollaries.
   - **Book VI** (to Attalus): Equal and unequal, similar and dissimilar conic sections and segments, and how to cut a given conic section inside a prescribed right cone.
   - **Book VII** (to Attalus): New propositions concerning conjugate diameters and the rectilinear figures described upon them, serving as :term:`diorismoi <diorismos>` for the determinate conic problems solved in **Book VIII** (now lost).

Preface to Book II (To Eudemus & Philonides)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. epigraph::

   "Apollonius to Eudemus, greeting... I am sending my son Apollonius to you with the second book of my conics. Read it carefully and communicate it to such others as are worthy of it. And if :person:`Philonides <philonides>` the geometer, whom I introduced to you in Ephesus, should ever happen to visit the neighbourhood of Pergamum, communicate it to him also."
