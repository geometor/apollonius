Notes — Textual Witnesses, Trilogy Context & Planimetry
=======================================================

1. Completion of the Heath Cambridge Greek Trilogy
--------------------------------------------------

:person:`T. L. Heath's <heath>` *Treatise on Conic Sections* (Cambridge University Press, 1896) was the **inaugural volume** of his monumental three-part Cambridge synthesis of Greek geometry:

1. **1896 — Apollonius of Perga**: *Treatise on Conic Sections* (``geometor/apollonius``)
2. **1897 / 1912 — Archimedes**: *The Works of Archimedes* + *The Method* (``geometor/archimedes``; :cite:`Heath 1897 <archimedes:heath1897>`)
3. **1908 / 1926 — Euclid**: *The Thirteen Books of Euclid's Elements* (``geometor/euclid``; :cite:`Heath 1908 <euclid:heath1908>`)

Heath explains in his Preface (*pp. vii–xii*) why modern symbolic-geometric notation is indispensable for Apollonius: in Book VII alone, literal word-for-word translation of compound ratio chains over twenty auxiliary segments obscures the dazzling algebraic symmetry that :person:`Zeuthen <zeuthen>` (:cite:`1886 <zeuthen:1886>`) revealed as ancient coordinate geometry.

2. Planimetric Severance from the 3D Cone
-----------------------------------------

Heath highlights (*pp. xcvii–xcviii*) one of Apollonius's deepest architectural decisions: **once the fundamental symptom (:term:`symptōma <symptoma>`) is derived from the 3D cone in I.11–14, Apollonius never appeals to the solid cone again in Books I–V or VII** (returning to the cone only in Book VI.28–33 when embedding a given conic into a prescribed right cone). Every subsequent theorem of tangents, asymptotes, poles and polars, foci, intersections, normals, and conjugate diameters is deduced purely in the **2D plane** from the area-application equation!

3. Prosopographical Note on Philonides & Pergamum
-------------------------------------------------

Apollonius's casual mention of :person:`Philonides <philonides>` in the Preface to Book II (*p. lxxii*) gained remarkable archaeological confirmation from the carbonized *Vita Philonidis* (PHerc. 1044) unearthed at Herculaneum, which confirms Philonides of Laodicea's studies with Eudemus and Dionysodorus and anchors the publication of Books I–II of the *Conics* squarely around **200–190 BCE**.
