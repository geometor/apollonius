Notes — Bridges to ``geometor/pappus`` & ``geometor/etc``
=========================================================

1. Triangle Centers Born Directly from Apollonius (``geometor/etc``)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Three flagship centers in :person:`Clark Kimberling's <kimberling:etc>` *Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers* (``geometor/etc``) arise directly from the two Apollonian locus and tangency theorems in Unit 7:

* **First & Second Isodynamic Points ``X(15)`` and ``X(16)``**: On reference triangle :g:`ABC` with side lengths :eq:`a, b, c`, the three **Apollonius Circles** (:eq:`PB : PC = c : b` through :g:`A`, :eq:`PC : PA = a : c` through :g:`B`, and :eq:`PA : PB = b : a` through :g:`C`) share the **Lemoine Axis** as common radical axis and intersect in two real points inverse in the circumcircle—``X(15)`` and ``X(16)``—whose pedal triangles are **equilateral**!
* **The Apollonius Point ``X(181)``**: Let :eq:`\Gamma_A, \Gamma_B, \Gamma_C` be the three **excircles** of triangle :g:`ABC`, and construct the unique outer **Apollonius (`CCC`) circle** :eq:`\Gamma` internally tangent to all three excircles at contact points :g:`A', B', C'`. The lines :g:`AA'`, :g:`BB'`, :g:`CC'` concur at **Kimberling's ``X(181)``**, with trilinear coordinates:

  .. math::

     X(181) = \frac{a(b + c)^2}{b + c - a} : \frac{b(c + a)^2}{c + a - b} : \frac{c(a + b)^2}{a + b - c}.

2. Fermat's Coordinate Geometry Born from *Plane Loci* (1636)
-------------------------------------------------------------

Just as :person:`Descartes <descartes>` discovered analytic geometry in 1637 while solving Apollonius's **Three- and Four-Line Locus** from *Conics* Book III, Pierre de Fermat independently discovered coordinate geometry (*Ad Locos Planos et Solidos Isagoge*, 1636) while reconstructing Apollonius's lost **Plane Loci** from :person:`Pappus <pappus>` Book VII—proving algebraically that every linear equation defines a straight line and every Circle of Apollonius :eq:`(x - x_A)^2 + (y - y_A)^2 = k^2\bigl[(x - x_B)^2 + (y - y_B)^2\bigr]` simplifies to a circle!
