Notes — Bridges to geometor/pappus & geometor/etc¶

1. Triangle Centers Born Directly from Apollonius (geometor/etc)¶

Three flagship centers in Clark Kimberling's Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers (geometor/etc) arise directly from the two Apollonian locus and tangency theorems in Unit 7:

  • First & Second Isodynamic Points ``X(15)`` and ``X(16)``: On reference triangle ABC with side lengths , the three Apollonius Circles ( through A, through B, and through C) share the Lemoine Axis as common radical axis and intersect in two real points inverse in the circumcircle—X(15) and X(16)—whose pedal triangles are equilateral!

  • The Apollonius Point ``X(181)``: Let be the three excircles of triangle ABC, and construct the unique outer Apollonius (`CCC`) circle internally tangent to all three excircles at contact points ABC. The lines AA, BB, CC concur at Kimberling’s ``X(181)``, with trilinear coordinates:

    \[X(181) = \frac{a(b + c)^2}{b + c - a} : \frac{b(c + a)^2}{c + a - b} : \frac{c(a + b)^2}{a + b - c}.\]

2. Fermat’s Coordinate Geometry Born from Plane Loci (1636)¶

Just as Descartes discovered analytic geometry in 1637 while solving Apollonius’s Three- and Four-Line Locus from Conics Book III, Pierre de Fermat independently discovered coordinate geometry (Ad Locos Planos et Solidos Isagoge, 1636) while reconstructing Apollonius’s lost Plane Loci from Pappus Book VII—proving algebraically that every linear equation defines a straight line and every Circle of Apollonius simplifies to a circle!
