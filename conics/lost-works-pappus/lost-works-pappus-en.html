Appendix — The Lost Analytic Treasury in Pappus Book VII¶

[Folio Heath 1896, Intro pp. lxviii, cxxv–clvi (PDF 76, 133–164); Pappus Synagoge VII]

Beyond the Conics, Apollonius of Perga authored six major two-book treatises forming the core of the ancient Domain of Analysis (Τόπος ἀναλυόμενος) preserved for posterity in Book VII of Pappus of Alexandria's Mathematical Collection (Synagoge, ed. Hultsch 1877; geometor/pappus). Five of these treatises were lost in Greek, while one (Cutting Off of a Ratio) survived in Arabic (Halley 1706).

1. On Tangencies (Περὶ ἐπαφῶν) — The Problem of Apollonius (CCC)¶

Pappus summarizes the famous ten contact problems of Apollonius’s two-book treatise On Tangencies: given any three geometric elements in a plane—each being a Point (`P`), a Straight Line (`L`), or a Circle (`C`)—construct by straightedge and compass a circle tangent to all three (PPP, PPL, PPC, PLL, PLC, PCC, LLL, LLC, LCC, and CCC). While PPP (IV.5) and LLL (IV.4) belong to Euclid's Elements, the crowning tenth problem `CCC` (Three Given Circles) admits up to eight distinct tangent circles:

Plate 11 — The Problem of Apollonius (`CCC`: All 8 Solutions). Given three mutually exterior circles C1C2C3 (bold), exact algebraic solution yields 8 tangent circles: the inner externally-tangent Soddy/Apollonius circle (gold solid), the outer internally-enclosing Apollonius circle (gold dashed, defining ETC X(181)), and three conjugate pairs of mixed-contact circles (teal and rose).

Reconstructed by François Viète in Apollonius Gallus (1600) by concentric radius dilation (reducing CCC to PCC) and by Gergonne via the radical center and pole-polar duality of the four similarity axes, the CCC configuration connects directly to geometor/elements and geometor/etc!

2. Plane Loci (Τόποι ἐπίπεδοι) — The Circle of Apollonius¶

In Book II of Plane Loci, Apollonius proved one of the most fertile theorems of Euclidean and triangle geometry:

Locus of Apollonius (Plane Loci II). Whereas the locus of a point P whose distances to two fixed points AB are equal () is the perpendicular bisector of AB, the locus of P whose distances bear any given unequal ratio () is a circle (the Circle of Apollonius), whose diameter HmathrminHmathrmex divides AB internally and externally in the ratio ([VI.3]).

3. The Remaining Four Analytic Treatises in Pappus VII¶

  1. Cutting Off of a Ratio (Περὶ λόγου ἀποτομῆς, 2 Books — extant in Arabic, ed. Halley 1706): Through a given point draw a straight line cutting off segments on two given lines (measured from given base points) bearing a prescribed ratio—exhaustively classified across 21 loci and diorismoi.

  2. Cutting Off of an Area (Περὶ χωρίου ἀποτομῆς, 2 Books — restored by Halley 1706): Same construction except the two intercepted segments must contain a given rectangle (hyperbolic/quadratic counterpart of Cutting Off of a Ratio).

  3. Determinate Section (Περὶ διωρισμένης τομῆς, 2 Books): Given four collinear points ABCD, find P on the line such that (or )—a complete 1D projective involution theory with exact extrema!

  4. Inclinations (Περὶ νεύσεων, 2 Books — Neusis): Construct by pure straightedge and compass a segment of given length between two given lines/circles verging through a given pole (e.g., inserting a given chord in a circle or rhombus).
