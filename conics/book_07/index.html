Book VII: Conjugate Diameter Theorems & Extrema¶

Propositions 123–147 [Classical VII. 1–51] — Apollonius of Perga (ed. T. L. Heath, 1896)

Book VII crowns ancient geometric algebra with Apollonius’s celebrated Theorems on Conjugate Diameters: the quadratic trace invariance () and the determinant parallelogram area invariance (). It then establishes the exhaustive series of inequality diorismoi constructed as the solvability machinery for the lost Book VIII.

Sections¶

1. Apollonius’s Conjugate Diameter Theorems [Props. 123–136 / VII. 1–32]

Proposition 129 [VII. 12, 13, 29, 30] — Apollonius’s First Theorem (Quadratic Trace Invariance). In every ellipse the sum, and in every hyperbola the difference, of the squares on any two conjugate diameters PP, DD is equal to the sum or difference respectively of the squares on the principal axes AA, BB:

\[PP'^2 + DD'^2 = AA'^2 + BB'^2 \quad (\text{ellipse}: a'^2 + b'^2 = a^2 + b^2),\]
\[PP'^2 - DD'^2 = AA'^2 - BB'^2 \quad (\text{hyperbola}: a'^2 - b'^2 = a^2 - b^2).\]
