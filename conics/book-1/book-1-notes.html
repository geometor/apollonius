Notes — Book I: Geometric Algebra, Order of Curves & Projective Frames¶

1. Why Apollonius Orders the Curves Parabola → Hyperbola → Ellipse¶

Modern textbooks routinely list ellipse, parabola, hyperbola by eccentricity (, , ). In Book I.11–13, however, Apollonius proves Parabola (`I.11`), then Hyperbola (`I.12`), then Ellipse (`I.13`):

  • In I.11, the cutting plane is parallel to a side of the axial triangle, giving the simplest one-parameter application .

  • In I.12 and I.13, the cutting plane is non-parallel to both sides; when the intersection P with the second generator falls above the apex A on the opposite nape (I.12, hyperbola), the auxiliary parallel AF falls outside the axial triangle, whereas for I.13 (ellipse) it falls inside, and both proofs pave the immediate transition to I.14 (opposite branches).
