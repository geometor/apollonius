3. Finding Diameters, Axes & Tangents by Compass and Straightedge [Props. 47–53 / II. 44–53]¶

Given a drawn conic section in a plane without its cone or parameters, let its diameter, center, axes, and tangents be found as follows:

  1. Finding a Diameter & Center (`II.44–45 [Props. 47–48]`): Draw two parallel chords and join their midpoints to obtain a diameter; intersect two non-parallel diameters to obtain the center C.

  2. Finding the Principal Orthogonal Axes (`II.46–48 [Props. 49–50]`): In a parabola, draw a chord perpendicular to the found diameter and bisect it to obtain the unique axis; in a central conic, describe a circle around C meeting the conic in KH—the lines through C bisecting KH and its supplement give the two orthogonal axes ([III.3]).

  3. Drawing Tangents from Any Point (`II.49 [Prop. 51]`): Given an external point T, find the contact points QQ by harmonic division on the diameter through T ( for central conics; for the parabola); at most two tangents can be drawn to the section from T.
