Greek & Arabic Textual Transmission
===================================

The survival of Apollonius's *Conics* across twenty-two centuries is one of the most dramatic chapters in the history of science:

1. **Alexandrian & Late-Antique Commentators (4th–6th c. CE)**:
   - :person:`Pappus of Alexandria <pappus>` (*c.* 320 CE) preserved 70+ lemmas to all eight Books of the *Conics* in *Synagoge* VII (:cite:`Hultsch 1877 <hultsch:1877>`).
   - :person:`Hypatia of Alexandria <hypatia>` (*d.* 415 CE) authored a lost Greek commentary on the *Conics*.
   - :person:`Eutocius of Ascalon <eutocius>` (*c.* 510 CE) edited **Books I–IV** with an extensive Greek commentary dedicated to Anthemius of Tralles. Because Byzantine scriptoria copied Eutocius's four-book school edition, **only Books I–IV survived in Greek** (preserved chiefly in *Codex Vaticanus Graecus 206*, 12th–13th c., edited critically by :person:`J. L. Heiberg <heiberg>` in :cite:`1891–1893 <heiberg:1891>`).

2. **Ninth-Century Baghdad Recovery of Books V–VII**:
   - In Abbasid Baghdad, the three brothers :person:`Banū Mūsā <banu-musa>` acquired a damaged seven-book Greek manuscript lacking Book VIII. Unable at first to decipher its corrupt diagrams and technical shorthand, Ahmad ibn Mūsā located in Damascus a copy of Eutocius's recension of Books I–IV.
   - Using Books I–IV as a Rosetta Stone, Hilāl ibn Abī Hilāl al-Ḥimṣī translated Books I–IV into Arabic, while the great geometer :person:`Thābit ibn Qurra <thabit-ibn-qurra>` (836–901 CE) translated **Books V–VII**—rescuing Apollonius's theories of normals, evolutes, similarity, and conjugate diameters from total oblivion! (Copied in 1024 CE by :person:`Ibn al-Haytham <ibn-al-haytham>`, and preserved in Bodleian MS *Marsh 667*.)

3. **Halley's 1710 Oxford Folio**:
   - After Golius brought the Arabic manuscript to Leiden and Edward Bernard began transcribing Bodleian *Marsh 667*, Savilian Professor :person:`Edmond Halley <halley>` mastered Arabic, published *De Sectione Rationis* (:cite:`1706 <halley:1706>`), and in **1710** printed at Oxford the monumental folio *Apollonii Pergaei Conicorum Libri Octo* (:cite:`Halley 1710 <halley:1710>`), reuniting Greek Books I–IV with Latin Books V–VII and a restored Book VIII.
